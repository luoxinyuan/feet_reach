# Proactive EE residual over frozen CEER

This task reuses the two-layer `HierarchicalRootCommand` / `RootStudentPPOPolicy`
framework. The low-level checkpoint is the final checkpoint from
`luoxinyuan-duke-university/gentle_humanoid/gentle_finetune_root_wrist_12`
(display name `3point_ceer (root_wrist_12_finetune)`). Its student actor, state/joint
estimators, normalization, action scales, filtering and delay remain frozen.

The high-level action is three raw values. The command sent to CEER is
`left_hand_nominal_xyz + 0.15 * tanh(action)` in the live root frame (meters).
Right-hand pose, left-hand orientation and root commands come from the reference.
The nominal target used for the reward is never modified by the residual.
References and initialization use `dataset/ee_reach/train`, generated by the
existing proactive EE Crocoddyl pipeline.

The high-level public observations contain the nominal left-hand target xyz,
angular velocity, projected gravity, joint history, low-level action history and
residual action history. A PointNet independently encodes 128 root-frame metric
points with validity masks into 64 features for each actor and critic. The cloud
is the existing synthetic noisy wall-surface cloud, refreshed at 25 Hz and
transformed at 50 Hz; it is not rendered depth or a SLAM implementation.
Teacher privilege describes robot/support state. The adaptation estimator predicts
the teacher latent from public proprioceptive observations; both actors directly
receive PointNet geometry.

Only two stages run: teacher PPO (4 billion aggregate environment steps) then
student adaptation (1 billion). Adaptation freezes actor/PointNet and normalizer,
and trains the privileged-state estimator, following the existing high-level
algorithm. There is no finetune stage. The whole-body joint estimator remains
inside the frozen CEER low-level, rather than being trained by the high-level.

The first reward version used proactive left-hand EE tracking weight 2.0,
contact force growth penalty weight 0.1, and unchanged proactive locomotion terms.
Root/joint/full-body reference tracking rewards were removed. That historical
penalty was `-0.1 * max((peak_positive_loading_rate - 500) / 500, 0)^2`; the current
wall-specific capped version is described below. There is no absolute hand-force
penalty or new residual-action regularizer. The original foot impact/slip/air-time,
joint-limit/velocity, action-rate and survival terms remain. Cumulative full-body
tracking-error termination is replaced by root-height and fall-over checks. CEER's
force-limit command is held at 30 N; this is a low-level input, not an added
high-level force penalty.

Run from the project root (CEER checkpoint downloaded to `.cache/ceer/checkpoint_final.pt`):

```bash
bash scripts/train_proactive_residual_pipeline.sh
```

Defaults: 8 GPUs, 4096 environments per GPU (32768 total), W&B project
`wall-foot-reach`, names `proactive-residual-ceer-v1-train` and
`proactive-residual-ceer-v1-adapt`. Override `LOW_CHECKPOINT`, `NUM_ENVS`, `NPROC`,
`TEACHER_FRAMES`, `ADAPT_FRAMES`, or `WANDB_RUN_NAME` as needed. Logs, resolved
configs and checkpoints are under `outputs/proactive-residual/<timestamp>`;
`latest_pipeline.txt` identifies the most recent launch. Each stage must finish
and produce exactly one final checkpoint before the next stage starts.

Validation scripts: `tests/test_root_student_pointnet.py` (CPU) and
`scripts/references/smoke_proactive_residual.py --phase train`, followed by
`--phase adapt --checkpoint artifacts/proactive_residual/smoke_train.pt` (Isaac Sim).
The latter verifies exact learned CEER weights, frozen parameters across updates,
observation order, target isolation, three-dimensional action decoding, PointNet
PPO gradients, finite training updates and public-only student inference.

## Contact requirement reward

Run `20261012_232749` added the first `contact_requirement` term while leaving
the EE tracking and impact penalties unchanged. A wall-filtered left-hand sensor
latches contact at 1 N across physics substeps. Contact was required when the
target was more than 5 mm behind the wall. With equal ±1 scores and weight 2.0,
the required state appeared on only 6–7% of training steps; the term's logged
mean magnitude was about 4% of EE tracking. The resulting policy did not contact
the wall in the seed-42 evaluation.

The new training configuration raises wall-present probability to 80%, samples
wall planes from x=0.38–0.44 m rather than 0.40–0.50 m, and uses scores of +1 for
required contact, −1.5 for missing required contact, and −1 for unexpected
contact, with weight 3.0. Based on the stored training target distribution, this
should raise required-contact exposure to about 16% of control steps. The existing
loading-rate impact penalty remains unchanged. New metrics split required hit,
required miss, and unexpected contact rates. The 5 mm target margin and 1 N
contact threshold are unchanged.

The new run `20261013_095443` completed in tmux session `contact-tradeoff-v2`
with 8 GPUs, 4096 environments per GPU, 4 billion teacher frames, and 1 billion
adaptation frames. Its final adapt logs report a 19.3% required-contact fraction,
98.1% hit rate among required steps, and 1.7% unexpected contact among
non-required steps. The contact requirement was learned in training. Checkpoints
and logs are under
[`outputs/proactive-residual/20261013_095443`](../../outputs/proactive-residual/20261013_095443).

## Low-level only wall control (zero residual)

Using the same residual checkpoint, target, wall position, and evaluation protocol,
the high-level residual actor was bypassed and its 3D residual fixed at zero. The
frozen low-level policy remained active. All three trials (seeds 42–44) contacted
the wall, at 0.14 s after the target command, with no stability failure. Filtered
hand-wall normal force peaked at 28.6–36.4 N (mean 32.0 N); hold-period steady
force was 20.7–21.9 N (mean 21.1 N). Mean hold tracking error was 96.6–122.5 mm,
so none reached the 3 cm tracking criterion. Force samples are logged at 50 Hz
(physics at 200 Hz).

The directly matched seed-42 residual trial did not contact the wall and measured
0 N, with 89.6 mm mean hold error. With zero residual, seed 42 contacted at 0.14 s,
peaked at 31.0 N, settled to 21.9 N, and had 113.6 mm mean hold error. Thus the
low-level-only condition contacts the wall, but this comparison does not establish
intentional force regulation; the high, variable contact force and poor target
tracking warrant treating it as incidental contact in this run.

## Delayed contact-miss cost and scale update

The delayed-reward version removes the persistent positive reward for required
contact. Once the target is more than 5 mm behind the wall, the policy gets 8
control steps (160 ms at 50 Hz) to approach. If contact is still absent, the
miss cost ramps linearly over 8 more steps. In the first delayed-penalty run,
the cap was 0.5 weighted reward units per step (−0.01 after the 20 ms timestep
scale). Its final adapt logs showed a mean episode contact-miss contribution of
−18.24 before timestep scaling (−0.36 after scaling), compared with −54.32 for
the loading-rate term (−1.09 after scaling). Thus missed contact cost only
reached about one third of the typical loading-rate cost.

The updated cap is 1.5 weighted units per step (maximum −0.03 after timestep
scaling), with the same grace, ramp duration, and 1 N contact threshold. At the
old policy's miss/contact rates, this triples the cumulative miss cost to an
estimated −1.09 per episode, matching the old loading-rate cost magnitude. This
is a fixed-policy calibration estimate; the new policy may change its behavior
and therefore the measured returns. Contact turns the miss cost off; if contact
is lost while still required, the cost resumes at the level set by elapsed time.
Unexpected contact retains a score of −3. In that historical run, the loading-
rate cost still used the old soft threshold 500 N/s and weight 0.1. There was no
absolute-force penalty.

The 160 ms grace and 160 ms ramp are based on the stored target clips: the
target first moves behind the wall around 0.74 s from clip start (10th–90th
percentile 0.54–0.98 s), and required-contact intervals last a median 2.14 s.
With the old 0.5 cap, 0.6 s at full miss cost matched one peak-step loading-rate
penalty estimated from the prior v2 evaluation. The new cap reaches that
equivalent in about 0.2 s at the cap, after the grace and ramp. New metrics
report late misses and required-state age alongside hit/miss rates.

## Contact impulse penalty (current configuration)

At the first expected wall contact (>1 N, with the target behind the wall),
integrate the filtered wall-normal force at physics rate for 100 ms. Apply one
episode-level cost after the window completes:
`-min(6 * (max(J_100ms - 2.0, 0) / 1.1)^2, 10)`, where `J_100ms` is in N·s.
The first 2.0 N·s are free (about 20 N sustained for 100 ms); 3.1 N·s gives a
raw score of −6, or −0.12 after the 20 ms control-step scaling. The cost caps at
−10 raw (−0.20 scaled). The delayed missing-contact penalty is unchanged, so
avoiding contact still incurs its existing cost. The pre-contact speed reward
term is no longer enabled in the current task config. Historical speed-reward
parameters and method remain available for old checkpoint compatibility.

The metric uses physics-rate samples (200 Hz in training), not the 20/50 Hz
downsampled diagnostic curves. The 100 ms duration smooths single-step force
spikes while retaining an episode-level contact-load signal. Training logs now
include mean completed impulse and mean raw impulse penalty.

## Zero-residual contact approach speed

The existing three-trial low-level-only wall evaluation (seeds 42–44) logs hand
position and filtered wall force at 50 Hz. At the first sample above 1 N, the
measured x velocity was −0.017, −0.029, and −0.043 m/s respectively, so the hand
was nearly stationary or moving slightly away at the sampled instant. The samples
40–80 ms earlier show approach velocities around +0.67 to +0.76 m/s. Since the
position-derived velocity and force are sampled every 20 ms, these numbers do not
resolve the instantaneous velocity at the exact collision onset; the robot was
already braking sharply by the first detected-contact sample.
